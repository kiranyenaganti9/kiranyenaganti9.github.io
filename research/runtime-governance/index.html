Evidence-Bound Runtime Governance for Autonomous Multi-Agent AI Systems

Policy enforcement, monitoring, and risk-based human escalation.

Manuscript · SSRN, October 17, 2026 · DOI 10.2139/ssrn.7622081 · ResearchGate
An AI agent can have permission to act and still lack the evidence to make a sound decision.

Permission does not settle the question

A leasing assistant may be fully authorized to use a scheduling system. The availability record it retrieved may still be stale, or another agent may have handed it an unverified assumption. A policy check tells you whether the action is allowed. It says nothing about whether the information behind it is adequate.

Three questions at execution time

01

Is the action permitted?

Check it against policy and the agent's authority.

02

What supports the decision?

Is the information relevant, current and sufficient? Are there gaps or contradictions?

03

Does a person need to decide?

If evidence or authority is insufficient, pause or escalate. Uncertainty is not approval.

An illustration

An agent is asked to confirm a tour against an availability record fetched half an hour ago. A governed workflow would re-fetch or route the decision to a person before committing, and log why. This is an illustration of the approach, not an experimental result.

From use case to research question

The leasing concept led to broader questions: how agents coordinate, how they use enterprise data, and how they recover when a model or data source fails. Those questions shaped the patent application on governed multi-agent workflows and the DataGovernAI prototype, where parts of the approach are tested in code.

Open questions

  • How evidence quality is defined and measured.
  • How escalation thresholds are chosen.
  • How conflicting sources are handled.
  • What governance checks cost in task completion and latency.
